# 0.5.9 preview

**Depth boost, now with the dual camera** (experimental, off by default, Perception page): the world outside is rendered with your eyes a little further apart, following where you look and changing slowly, while the cockpit, its displays and its lighting stay at your real eye distance. The HUD stays where DCS draws it. Before, Depth boost widened the eye distance for the whole view and returned it to normal when you looked back into the cockpit; that step is gone. Realistic and Enhanced stay; the Gradual/Predictive choice and its Alt+Shift+F9 key are gone (the depth now always changes smoothly). Alt+Shift+F10 still turns it off and on in flight, and the in-headset panel shows "(dual camera)" on the Depth boost line.

**Own aircraft at true depth** (on by default with Depth boost, experimental): in helicopters and two-seaters, the other crew station or the front seat outside your cockpit keeps its real distance instead of looking closer. It keeps the multi-threaded G-buffer running in views where your own aircraft fills the picture (the F-14's rear seat looking back, for example).

**Depth boost without Engine Optimizations**: Depth boost needs DCS Control's engine module, which now also runs in a Perception-only mode: with Engine Optimizations off it is installed for Depth boost alone and none of the optimizations, the test suite or the engine keys run. Far depth still needs Engine Optimizations.

**Feature guide** (new page, right under Overview): every feature in a few lines, with what you get, what to expect, what it costs, what it needs and its in-flight key, and whether it is on in your profile.

**Tidier pages**: Eye distance moved to the Perception page, next to the features that build on it; Periphery stability moved to the Quad Views page, next to the other periphery settings; the Diagnostics page is now called Troubleshooting, so it is not mixed up with Checks. Texts across the app were reviewed: shorter, and no internal names where they don't help. The Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 page shortcuts follow the new order (Ctrl+2 is the Feature guide).

**Fix (GitHub #3)**: switching Engine Optimizations off and back on in flight (Alt+Shift+F11) could turn the multi-threaded G-buffer off for the rest of the session. A late re-record of part of a frame could read objects DCS was already recycling; it now stops as soon as its frame is done, a fault there costs only that piece of one frame, and switching back on waits for the next full frame before the recorders start.

# 0.5.8 preview

**Far depth** (off by default; Perception page): distant aircraft, ships and terrain get more depth, between about 50 m and 2 km, while the cockpit stays exactly as it is. The bundled Quad Views shifts each eye's image using the depth DCS drew, which Engine Optimizations measure; under 0.1 ms per frame. Subtle, Default or Strong. The OSD shows "Far depth: ON".

**In-headset panel without frame generation**: the status panel (Alt+Shift+F12) no longer needs frame generation. Turn on *In-headset panel* on the new **OSD** page, which also lists every in-flight key: OFXR is loaded only to draw it and passes your frames through untouched. The panel now shows the eye distance DCS renders with Depth boost and a Periphery stability line.

**Perception page** (renamed from Pupil shift): Pupil shift, Depth boost and Periphery stability together, with shorter descriptions. **First-start tour**: a short tour shows where everything is; take it again from the Diagnostics page.

**Pupil shift**: the cockpit no longer jitters up and down. The gaze that moves the pupils is smoothed against the eye tracker's noise (more on a noisier tracker), follows smooth eye movements without steps and holds through blinks.

**Eye distance** (Headset runtime card): DCS now renders with the eye distance your headset measured or was set to (Pimax Play, SteamVR), the realistic one, and DCS's custom IPD is turned off while a profile is applied (Back to stock DCS turns it on again). Choose *DCS's custom IPD* to keep the old behaviour, or *Set here* for a value of your own.

**Periphery stability** (off by default; Perception page, bundled Quad Views only): steadies the shimmer that the enlarged periphery shows on thin lines (canopy frame, power lines, horizon) outside the sharp focus area, where the eye is most sensitive to flicker. Each periphery frame is blended with the previous one, moved with your head, with no effect inside the focus area; a jump of the eyes, a pause or a large head turn starts it over. One switch and a Strength slider. It costs about 0.03 ms of GPU time for both eyes. Offline it cut the periphery's frame-to-frame flicker by about 30 % (3X) to 40 % (2X) between 28 and 45 degrees. On scenery that moves on its own, such as the ground while the aircraft rolls, it can leave a faint trail. How it works and the measurements: docs/PERCEPTION.md.
**Depth boost** (off by default, on the Perception page): while you look outside, both eyes are rendered gradually further apart (up to ×1.4 of your eye distance by default), so a wingman, a tanker or a target shows more stereo depth; when you look back into the cockpit it returns to your normal eye distance within about a second. Where you look is measured from the angle between your two eyes' gaze, read from the eye-tracked Quad Views focus views, in the Pupil shift layer, which now runs for either feature (each has its own switch). While the boost is up the world looks somewhat smaller; keep it off for landings for now. In-flight switch Alt+Shift+F10; the diagnostic panel shows the current scale. It acts only once it has seen you look both into the cockpit and outside; when both focus areas follow one shared gaze (the bundled Quad Views does this), it stays at the normal scale and the page says so. Not yet flown: docs/PERCEPTION.md has the design, the offline validation and the test plan.

**Depth boost: Realistic and Enhanced, scene depth.** Depth boost now has two modes. **Realistic** (the default) keeps normal depth up to 150 m, where the headset already shows depth as well as your eyes would, and adds only the depth its pixels cannot show farther out (up to ×1.25 from 400 m): a wingman or a rejoin keeps its true distance. **Enhanced** is the previous behaviour (more depth from 3 m, ×1.4 from 50 m): more depth, not realistic. With Engine Optimizations the engine module measures the distance you look at in DCS's depth buffer around your gaze; edges such as the canopy frame, the glareshield or an MFD bezel never switch the boost, the HUD keeps normal depth, and a change happens only as your eyes land after a jump. On Pimax the layer also reads each eye's own gaze from the runtime's PVR client. Without Engine Optimizations Realistic stays at normal depth (the page says so) and Enhanced works on your eyes' vergence as before. docs/FIXATION_DEPTH.md has the rules and their validation.

**Perception features are marked EXPERIMENTAL**: Pupil shift, Periphery stability, Far depth and Depth boost carry the tag on the Perception page. They are all off by default and still being tuned; tell us how they look in your aircraft.

**Far depth keeps the HUD out**: the HUD and symbology that aircraft such as the F-16 draw after DLSS are now found in the HDR image, before and after they are drawn, so they are never shifted with the world behind them.

**Last flight: eye tracking**: when the bundled Quad Views reports that eye tracking did not reach it, the Last flight page says so and suggests restarting the headset in Pimax Play (desktop Tobii software can hold the headset's tracker).

**Fixes**:
- Open Sboys tool and Back to stock DCS no longer stay greyed out after another command.
- Cancelling Import, or Back to stock DCS, no longer brings back an older draft over unsaved changes.
- The guided setup asks about another program's dxgi.dll before it launches, like Launch DCS, and a cancelled guided setup leaves nothing behind.
- Back to stock DCS: a failed write of options.lua no longer forgets an original setting; another program's dxgi.dll reached through two paths (a junction, a subst drive) is recognised as one file; a damaged backup no longer blocks every later Apply.
- Ctrl+Alt+F8 to F12 and Page Up/Down, with or without Shift, are refused for every in-flight key while Engine Optimizations run (the module's own keys).
- Alt+F4 no longer closes DCS Control in the middle of Launch, Apply or Back to stock DCS.
- An options.lua outside Saved Games\DCS\Config gets a warning; the write-access check says the same thing in Checks and in the profile check.
- The OSD fields are greyed out for the monitor route; Checks link every key and range problem to its setting; the tour closes with Esc.
- The release no longer contains the build machine's folder names (Quad Views' compiled-in source paths and symbol path); every file of both packages is checked.

- Pupil shift layer: a NaN focus FOV no longer reaches DCS as a NaN eye position; views with a NaN FOV are restored at `xrEndFrame` too.
- OFXR patch 0017: the panel's Pupil shift line carries Depth boost.

# 0.5.7 preview

**Engine optimizations on a GPU-bound PC**: when the graphics card is the limit (a fast CPU with a high headset resolution), the multi-threaded shadows and G-buffer used a little GPU time for no gain. The module now steps them down while the GPU is the limit and back up when the CPU is, keeping each step only when a paired 20-second test shows it raises FPS by more than 2 %. The image never changes.

**Easier to tell what happens**:
- **Test suite** (Run test suite, or Ctrl+Alt+F11 in DCS) now compares all the optimizations off against on, in about 3 minutes, and stops with a plain message when it sees no headset frames.
- The module's log says at start which GPU, driver, VRAM, OpenXR runtime and layers, streaming apps (Virtual Desktop and others) and view layout it found, then every 10 seconds the FPS, frame times and whether the frame was CPU- or GPU-bound, and which optimizations are on, idle or off and why.
- The diagnostic package keeps the start of a long log as well as its end, so the start-up lines survive a long flight.
- **Multi-threaded shadows and G-buffer unavailable on an NVIDIA card?** Checks now warn before the flight when NVIDIA Control Panel's *Threaded optimization* is Off for DCS (a common DCS VR tweak: with it off the driver gives DCS no command lists) and say where to set it back to Auto. The app reads the setting and never changes it. When the module still cannot use them, the DCS Engine page names the reason: that setting, DCS running on integrated graphics, or a program hooked into DCS.

**Fix**: the Pupil shift page and the diagnostic package can read the Pupil shift log while DCS runs (the layer kept it locked).

**Docs**: install, uninstall and updates described for the setup; technical notes on the setup, the uninstaller and the updater.

# 0.5.6 preview

**One-file setup**: `DcsControl-0.5.6-preview-Setup.exe` installs DCS Control for your Windows user, in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\Programs\DcsControl` or a folder you choose (it shows the space needed and free), with the Start menu and desktop shortcuts you tick and an entry in Settings → Apps. **Uninstall DCS Control** (Settings → Apps, or `Uninstall DCS Control.exe` in the folder) is a window too: it can put DCS back to stock first and, if you ask, delete your profiles and settings. No administrator rights, no console windows; WebView2 and the Visual C++ runtime come from Microsoft when they are missing, and an installed copy is updated in place (a failed update rolls back). It waits until DCS and DCS Control are closed. The ZIP stays available for a portable copy.

Update downloads the setup, checks its signature against the release key built into the app (a file that does not match is deleted and nothing is installed), closes DCS Control, installs and starts the new version. It refuses while DCS runs. From an unzipped copy the update installs DCS Control in the standard folder and says that the old folder can be deleted.
- The download can be cancelled, stops by itself when nothing arrives for a minute, and never starts while Launch DCS, Apply or Back to stock DCS is working. The setup must be the version announced, and a failed update gives you back the DCS Control you had.
- The check asks GitHub at most every 6 hours (30 minutes after a failed check) and never sends anything about your PC; it can be turned off on the Diagnostics page (**Check for updates when DCS Control starts**), where **Check now** asks at once.
- Without internet nothing changes: no update is shown.

**What's new**: the first start after an update shows that version's release notes once; click the version number at the bottom of the left rail to see them again.

# 0.5.5 preview

**Terrain prefetch fix moved into Engine Optimizations**: the fix for DCS's terrain workers, which ask Windows to prefetch the same memory ranges about a million times a second, now runs inside the engine optimizations module instead of a separate DLL. It is an option on the Engine Optimizations page (Off, Observe, Skip repeats; Skip by default) and no longer part of CPU Boost. Measured in an A/B: about 4 % less DCS CPU, more on CPU-bound scenes.
- **No more files in DCS's install folder unless DLSS 5 is used**: without DLSS 5 or Foveated SR, nothing goes to DCS's `bin` folder (no `dxgi.dll` loader, no `dxgi2.dll`, no `DcsControlPrefetchFix.ini`), so Optimizations only never needs write access to Program Files.
- **Works from Steam and shortcuts** like the rest of the module: its settings are in `DcsQvCull.ini` ([Prefetch]), its statistics in `Saved Games\DCS\Scripts\DcsQvCull\DcsQvCullPrefetch.log`, read by the Last flight card.
- **Migration is automatic**: saved profiles keep their setting, and the next Launch DCS (or Apply) removes the `dxgi.dll` and `dxgi2.dll` an earlier version put in `bin` and puts back your own `dxgi.dll` if you had one.

**ReShade (or another dxgi.dll mod) keeps working with DLSS 5**: Checks names it, and Launch DCS asks once. Keep it working loads it after the DLSS 5 loader as dxgi2.dll; Set it aside keeps it off while the profile is applied. It is backed up either way and Back to stock DCS puts it back. Before, it was replaced without a question beforehand.

# 0.5.4 preview

**DCS under Program Files works**: Windows protects Program Files, so Launch DCS stopped with "Access to the path … is denied" when DCS was installed there. DCS Control now checks before writing anything and, when a folder it needs is protected, asks Windows once (its own prompt) to give your account Modify permission on that folder only, then carries on. Back to stock DCS does the same. Checks shows it as "Write access to game folders" with a Grant access button, and names Windows Security's Controlled folder access when it is on.

**Sturdier file writes**: a file an antivirus, Windows Search or OneDrive holds for a moment no longer stops Launch or Back to stock DCS (the write waits up to about 2 s), and a file you made read-only (options.lua) is updated and stays read-only. Windows is asked for permission only when a write is actually refused, with one prompt for all folders, and never when the block is an antivirus, Controlled folder access or a read-only or network drive (those are named instead).

**Pupil shift**: with DCS's custom IPD on, DCS reset each eye's sideways position after the layer, so the pupil's sideways move (the main part of the correction) never reached it. The layer now applies the same IPD itself and DCS's own option is off while the profile is applied (Back to stock DCS turns it on again). Straight ahead is taken between the two eyes' views, so headsets whose views are canted outwards no longer shift both pupils towards the nose. The page says what Pupil shift corrects and what not, and shows the measured cant and the eye distances.

The shadow cache no longer turns itself off for the session when an antivirus holds a file for a moment. On graphics drivers without D3D11 command lists (common on AMD) the multi-threaded shadows and G-buffer are reported as not supported instead of failed; on CPUs with 8 logical cores or fewer the shadows use one helper thread.
- Pupil shift: the switch's beep can't outlive the layer; a non-number in its settings file is ignored. Quad Views: a saccade right after a pause no longer reuses the previous saccade's look-ahead. The prefetch fix also catches lookups made through KernelBase. The launcher passes on "Run: Minimized".
- The app:

# 0.4.2 preview

**Eye-tracked focus with frame generation** (Quad Views page, both on): DCS places the focus area once per rendered frame and, with frame generation, the image reaches the headset 55–88 ms later, so after a quick glance the eye could land outside the sharp area. The bundled Quad Views now detects saccades from the gaze's angular speed and, while one lasts (and for 40 ms after), extends the focus area along the eye's motion by its velocity times the display latency (25 / 55 / 85 ms without frame generation, in 2× and in Auto/3×, at most 0.35 of the view). At rest a 0.5° deadzone keeps the focus still against tracker noise. No GPU cost: the focus view keeps its pixel count. Offline tests simulate saccades at 2× and 3× frame times, fixation noise and blinks (tests/quadviews-gaze); the Quad Views log counts saccades and how often each acted.

**Pause the desktop Tobii eye tracker** (CPU Boost page → Flight helpers, off by default): with a Tobii Eye Tracker 5 or 4C on the same PC, Tobii Experience also opens the Pimax Crystal's Tobii eye chip (its log lists both devices) and Pimax eye tracking stops working. While DCS runs, the boost helper (elevated, one UAC prompt) stops the desktop tracker's services and starts them again when DCS exits; a helper ended early leaves a marker so the next one starts them. The headset's own runtimes are never touched.

**Back to stock DCS** (formerly Restore originals) on the **Stock DCS** page (formerly Recovery): the same one-step undo of every file and setting DCS Control changed, with a clearer name. Until it is used, the app's files stay active also when DCS is started from Steam (the Engine Optimizations hook in Saved Games and the dxgi.dll loader in bin).

**Engine Optimizations page (CPU, off by default)**: a native module, DcsQvCull, loaded by DCS from a Lua hook in `Saved Games\DCS\Scripts` (nothing in the DCS install changes), removes work from DCS's render thread without changing the image. The streaming timer cache answers the clock read DCS's DX11 backend makes on every texture bind (only to timestamp texture-streaming use) from a value refreshed every millisecond: +5 to +6 % FPS. The culling partition boost splits DCS's per-model culling work into 16 chunks instead of 12, so the render thread waits less for the slowest one: +2 % FPS, p95 −3 %. Eight more, each measured on its own, among them: per-thread blocks for the model data allocator (+6.2 % FPS, p95 −9 %), texture-streaming request dedupe (+2.3 %), and the effect constant-buffer skip with a statistics counter without a lock (+3.6 % together). The culling partition can also follow each object's measured culling cost (+2.75 % ±0.7, p95 −4 %). Per-thread blocks for the per-frame memory heap add +1.5 % (culling −9 %), and the task queue's frame-start drain reads the cached clock (render-thread drain 2.9 → 1.6 ms per frame while the camera moves fast). Shadow caster instancing draws identical shadow casters with one instanced draw per cascade, using instanced variants of DCS's own shadow shader compiled in the background (texel-identical in a live same-frame comparison, +2.8 % FPS), and skips the shadow texture sets those shaders never read (97.5 % of them; depth-identical); the grouping is planned off the render thread (+1.9 % FPS, texel-identical). Multi-threaded shadows record the shadow cascades' draws on 8 worker threads that DCS replays (texel-identical live, +3 to +5 % FPS, p95 −4 to −7 %, total CPU +4 to 7 % as the work moves off the render thread). A redundant state filter skips the graphics state DCS's renderer sets again although it is already set (about 228,000 calls a frame; verified exact live, about +0.5 to +1 % FPS, CPU −1.2 %). A multi-threaded G-buffer records DCS's G-buffer model draws on worker threads in DCS's exact order, which DCS replays (pixel-identical live, +21.7 % ±1.8 and +23.7 % ±2.2 FPS on its own). All together about +60 % FPS (+59.7 % ±3.6: 37.1 → 59.2 FPS), frame time −37.5 %, p95 −39 %, process CPU about unchanged, GPU busy 59 → 92 % (with everything on the GPU becomes the limit), measured with the module's own in-game A/B test (24 alternating 3 s blocks, 95 % confidence, the in-flight switch turning every optimization off, identical rendered objects off and on; F2 external view of a busy airfield) on a CPU-bound airfield in VR with Quad Views and no frame generation; not yet measured with DLSS 5 and frame generation. After a DCS update the module finds its hook sites again by signature (each one checked against the bytes it expects, otherwise left off with a warning), so the optimizations keep working when DCS only moved code around. A configurable in-flight key (Alt+Shift+F11 by default) turns both off and back on to compare. **Round, soft focus area** (Quad Views): the border between the sharp focus area and the periphery stayed visible because upstream Quad Views fades the focus view only from 100 % down to 50 % and then cuts it off (`max(0.5, s.x * s.y)`), a 50 % step all around a rectangle. The bundled Quad Views now has `focus_view_shape` (DCS Control writes 2): the focus area is the circle (ellipse) inscribed in the focus view and fades out radially with a smooth curve over the width **Focus edge blending** sets, down to exactly nothing at its edge. With Pimax Play as the source and its Transition Mode Off the blending is 0, a sharp edge.

**Faster frame generation, less VRAM** (OFXR fork patch 0012, Cheeky): OFXR's composition samples both frames through the texture unit, 15 % less GPU time per pair at Crystal Super resolution with the same or lower error in the quality fixtures; 3X composes both generated frames in one pass (bit-identical, 3–5 % less); each eye's pair takes two submissions and a queue wait fewer. Measurements and what was looked at and left out, with the reasons: Performance, "Optimization round".

**Lighter on DCS's render thread**: OFXR's flight log no longer writes the per-image swapchain calls (about 50 lines per DCS frame, two thirds of the log, each a synchronous write on DCS's render thread) unless one took 250 µs or more or failed; the log cap is 256 MB, so a flight log no longer starts over after about 8 minutes. **Video memory across missions** (Cheeky): DCS never releases its DLSS features; every mission load creates a new set and the previous mission's stay alive until DCS exits (flight 2026-10-06: 0 releases in the whole log, 16 live features at the second mission). **Smooth mouse cursor** (Framegen page, off by default until flown): DCS's VR mouse cursor is a small sprite that optical flow cannot follow, so generated frames showed it faded between its old and new place. CPU Boost (opt-in, off by default): while DCS runs, a separate `DcsVr.Cli boost` process raises DCS to the chosen priority (Above normal by default) and gives it every CPU, confines the VR runtime/headset services and listed background apps to the slower cores, optionally closes listed apps, and restores exactly what it changed when DCS exits or on Ctrl+C.
